## Module 17 — Plant Health
## Diagnosis
## 1. Diagnosing Plant Problems From
## Leaves
Leaves are one of the most informative diagnostic organs of a plant. Changes in leaf color, shape,
size, texture, surface, growth pattern, and distribution can provide clues about nutrient deficiencies,
toxicities, diseases, insect pests, herbicide injury, water stress, salinity, environmental stress, and
- ther disorders.
However, a leaf symptom by itself is rarely sufficient for a definitive diagnosis.
Leaf symptom→Clue→Differential diagnosis→Confirmation 
The correct approach is not:
“Yellow leaf = nitrogen deficiency.”
Instead:
Symptom+Pattern+Plant age+Crop stage+Environment+History+Soil/Plant test→Diagnosis 

1. What Is Plant Health Diagnosis?
Plant health diagnosis is the systematic process of identifying the cause of abnormal plant growth,
appearance, development, or function.
Possible causes include:

Biotic causes
Living organisms:
- Fungi
- Oomycetes
- Bacteria
- Viruses
- Viroids

- Phytoplasmas
- Nematodes
- Insect pests
- Mites
- Other pests

Abiotic causes
Non-living factors:
- Nutrient deficiency
- Nutrient toxicity
- Drought
- Waterlogging
- Salinity
- Sodicity
- Heat
- Cold
- Frost
- Hail
- Herbicide injury
- Fertilizer burn
- Soil pH problems
- Root-zone problems
- Air pollutants
- Mechanical injury
Therefore:
Plant problem=Biotic+Abiotic possibilities

7. Nutrient Mobility and Leaf Symptoms
Nutrient mobility helps explain symptom location.

Mobile nutrients
Examples:
- Nitrogen
- Phosphorus
- Potassium
- Magnesium
When deficient, plants can often move these nutrients from older tissues to younger tissues.
Therefore:
Mobile nutrient deficiency→Older leaves often show symptoms first 

Relatively immobile nutrients
Examples include:
- Calcium
- Boron
- Iron
Deficiency often appears in:
Young leaves+Growing points 
because nutrients cannot be efficiently redistributed from old tissues.

57. The Five Most Important Questions
When diagnosing from leaves, ask:

1. What exactly changed?
Color, shape, tissue, surface?

2. Where did it start?
Old leaves, young leaves, margin, veins, spots?

3. How is it distributed?
Random, patchy, rows, edges?

4. How quickly did it develop?
Sudden or gradual?

5. What happened recently?
- Fertilizer?
- Irrigation?
- Herbicide?
- Weather?
- Pest outbreak?
- Disease history?
These five questions can eliminate many incorrect diagnoses.

75. Final Conceptual Takeaway
The most important skill in plant health diagnosis is not memorizing that a particular symptom

The fundamental distinction is:

Do not treat the symptom until you have investigated the cause.

The stem is the plant axis that supports leaves, flowers and fruits and connects the root system with

Major functions include:

- Mechanical support
- Transport of water and minerals
- Transport of photosynthates
- Production and support of leaves
- Production/support of flowers and fruits

Roots are one of the most important but frequently overlooked organs for plant-health diagnosis.
Many above-ground symptoms—yellowing, wilting, stunting, nutrient deficiency-like symptoms, poor
flowering, and premature death—can actually originate from problems in the root system.
A useful diagnostic principle is:
Root problem→Water/nutrient uptake↓→Above-ground symptoms ground\ symptoms  
Therefore, when a plant shows unexplained leaf or stem symptoms, examining the roots can reveal
the actual cause.

1. What Is the Root System?
The root system is the below-ground portion of a plant responsible primarily for:
- Anchorage
- Water absorption
- Mineral nutrient absorption
- Storage
- Transport
- Interaction with soil microorganisms
- Hormonal/signaling functions
- Symbiotic relationships
- Vegetative propagation in some plants
The basic functional relationship is:
Root system↔Soil environment↔Shoot

- Soil moisture
- Soil oxygen
- Soil pH
- Salinity
- Soil compaction

- Nematodes
- Nutrient availability
- Toxic substances
- Temperature
- Soil structure

Soil is a dynamic natural body composed of mineral particles, organic matter, water, air and

Soil=Mineral Matter+Organic Matter+Water+Air+Biota 
Soil performs several functions:
- Supports roots
- Stores water
- Supplies nutrients
- Provides oxygen to roots
- Hosts microorganisms
- Provides habitat for soil organisms
- Stores carbon
- Filters and transforms substances
- Supports nutrient cycling

Heavy feeding can produce:

- Leaf curling
- Distortion
- Stunting
- Shoot deformation
The diagnosis becomes stronger when deformation occurs together with:
- Aphids
- Honeydew
- Sooty mold
- Ant activity
But aphid presence alone does not prove they caused the deformation.

Young tissue affected

55. Final One-Read Summary
Plant deformation is an abnormal change in the normal shape, size, structure or development of
a plant organ. It can occur because of biological agents such as insects, mites, viruses, phytoplasmas,
fungi, bacteria and nematodes, or because of abiotic factors such as drought, waterlogging, salinity,
temperature extremes, nutrient imbalance, herbicide injury, mechanical damage or genetics.
The fundamental mechanism is:
Normal growth→Growth/development disturbance→Abnormal cell division/expansion/
differentiation→Deformation 
Important examples:
Aphids→Leaf curling/distortion 
Virus→Mosaic+Distortion+Stunting 
Phytoplasma→Little leaf+Witches′ broom+Phyllody  Nematodes→Root galls/deformation 
Herbicide→Curling+Twisting+Strapping+Abnormal growth 
Water stress→Abnormal cell expansion/growth  Poor pollination/fertilization→Abnormal fruit development 
The complete diagnostic chain is:

Final memory
Deformation tells you that normal plant development has been disturbed, but it does
not tell you why. Identify the affected organ and type of deformation, compare young
and old tissues, look for insects and pathogen signs, examine roots and soil, consider
water, nutrition, temperature and herbicide history, study the field pattern and
progression, and confirm the cause before selecting a treatment.
